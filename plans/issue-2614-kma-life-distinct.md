# Plan for each stored life-index content once

Task declaration: `server2/config/tasks/KMA-LIFE-DISTINCT.json`. Based on master after #2924 (PR-Z) and #2926 (the restart acceptance). Design: revision 3 of `reports/sdlc/issue-2614-kma-life-distinct/design.md` (not tracked), approved after three design reviews.

1. Tests first, against the unchanged read and acquirer: the GET counts, the stated difference, the identical acquisition (no PUT, no own publication, no read after it, the overlay) and the restart over identical acquisitions fail; the fold oracle and the exclusion and error guards pass, which checks the oracle.
2. `Reader::listed_groups` keeps each group's entries; `Reader::distinct` and `Reader::all_distinct` restore each distinct content once for the life read; `Reader::stored_twin` finds a stored group with an acquisition's content. The wide restore loses its only reader and goes.
3. `LiveLife`: `CHECK_BUDGET`, the provider time apart from the owned time, the owner deadline, the check before publication and `LifeOutcome::Stored`. `Flights::join_owned` and `run_owned` give the owned work its deadline; the flight handles `Stored` without counting a publication; the overlay keeps a `Stored` entry until a committed applied set holds its stored group.
4. Guards: the per-partition equality with `lookup`, the publication paths, the stalled check, the slot near the owned cap and the provider time; temporary mutations of the gate, the provider deadline, the overlay rules and the order are each caught by a test.
5. Documents: the S15b-3b life-index input, the restart race and the restart acceptance in `docs/architecture/server2.md`.
6. Local checks (format, clippy, placement, artifact policy, lib, domestic and workspace tests); cross-provider PR review and AK's merge decision follow, then the EC2 re-measurement.
